Almost every guide to sourcing for Amazon FBA is written by a software company or a former seller. This one is written from the factory side — the perspective you never get to see. Here is what actually happens when an FBA seller contacts a leather manufacturer, where those conversations go wrong, and what the numbers really look like.

The honest summary

  • Leather is a hard first category for FBA. Margins are thinner than the courses suggest and quality variance is punishing in reviews.
  • Low MOQ is the whole game for a first product. Ours start at {{pg:moq-lowest}} pieces; most Chinese factories start at 300–500.
  • Prep and labelling is not an afterthought — FNSKU placement, polybag warnings and carton labels have to be specified before production, not after.
  • Budget 10–14 weeks from first contact to sellable inventory on a first order.

What FBA Sellers Should Know Before Contacting a Manufacturer

We receive a lot of first-time FBA enquiries. The ones that turn into good long-term accounts look different from the ones that stall, in fairly predictable ways.

What a useful first message contains

  • The specific product — "a bifold wallet in full-grain leather, roughly 11 × 9.5 cm, 8 card slots" rather than "leather goods".
  • Target quantity — even a rough one. Quantity determines price, and without it we are guessing.
  • Target landed cost, if you have one. Telling us your number is not weakness; it saves both of us three rounds of email.
  • Destination market — it determines compliance testing and labelling.
  • Whether you need FBA prep — say so at enquiry stage, not after the sample is approved.
The message that gets a slow reply

"Please send price list and catalogue, MOQ, best price." There is no price list for private label — the price is a function of your specification. A manufacturer reading fifty enquiries a week will prioritise the one that shows a real product decision has been made. That is not gatekeeping; it is that a specific question can actually be answered.

What we wish more sellers understood

Sampling is not a formality. It is the only point at which a problem is cheap to fix. Sellers who rush to skip sampling to save two weeks are the ones who later discover a construction issue across 500 units.

Photographs cannot substitute for swatches. Screen colour is unreliable and photographs carry no information about temper — how the leather folds and drapes. We send swatches free for exactly this reason.

The Full Process: Concept to FBA-Ready Shipment

  1. Enquiry and specification

    You send the product, quantity and market. We return feasibility, a leather recommendation and an indicative price. 2–4 days.

  2. Swatch approval

    Free leather swatches couriered to you. 5–8 days including transit.

  3. Sampling

    Physical pre-production sample. 10–14 days plus courier. Charged at cost, credited against your production order.

  4. Sample revision

    Most first orders need one revision round. Budget for it. 7–10 days.

  5. Compliance testing (if required)

    CPSIA or Prop 65 batch testing through SGS or Intertek where your category and market require it. 7–14 days, quoted separately.

  6. Production

    20–30 days for small leather goods, 35–50 for bags and outerwear.

  7. FBA prep and labelling

    FNSKU labels, polybagging, carton labelling, box content compliance. Done in-factory during packing, not as a separate step.

  8. Freight and customs

    Sea 25–35 days to US east coast. Customs clearance 3–7 days. Then FBA receiving, which is typically 3–10 days.

Total for a first order: 10–14 weeks realistically. Repeat orders on an approved design compress to 6–8 weeks because sampling is already done.

FBA Packaging, Labelling & Prep We Handle

Amazon's requirements are specific and unforgiving, and a rejected shipment at the fulfilment centre is expensive to fix from another continent. We handle prep in-factory:

  • FNSKU labels — applied to each unit, covering any existing barcode so only one scannable code remains.
  • Polybagging — with the required suffocation warning on any bag with an opening of 5 inches or more, and the bag transparent enough for the label to scan.
  • "Sold as set" labelling for multipacks, so a bundle is not broken up as individual units.
  • Carton labelling — FBA shipment labels plus your carton ID labels, correctly placed.
  • Carton weight and dimension limits — standard cartons kept under 50 lb / 25 kg.
  • Box content information — 2D barcodes or a manifest, so cartons are not flagged for manual processing fees.
Send us the requirements, not a summary

Amazon's prep and label specifications change periodically and differ by marketplace. Send us your actual shipment plan and label PDFs from Seller Central rather than a description of them. We would rather work from the source document than reconstruct it from a paraphrase.

Direct-to-FBA or via a prep centre?

We can ship directly to Amazon fulfilment centres, and for established sellers with a stable product that is the cheapest route. For a first order, we usually suggest shipping to your own address or a third-party prep centre first. It costs a little more, and it means you inspect the goods before Amazon does. Once you have seen one production run, ship direct.

Common Mistakes FBA Sellers Make

  1. Choosing a category on search volume alone

    High volume in leather goods means competing with established brands on a product where reviews punish quality variance harder than in most categories. Differentiated beats popular.

  2. Ordering too much on the first run

    The most expensive mistake we watch repeatedly. Order the minimum, validate the listing, then scale. Our minimums exist precisely so you can do this.

  3. Skipping the sample to save two weeks

    Two weeks saved against a five-figure inventory risk is not a trade worth making.

  4. Treating "genuine leather" as a quality claim

    It is a grade meaning the lower hide layers after splitting. Listing it as a premium feature invites reviews that say otherwise.

  5. Leaving prep requirements until after sampling

    Packaging affects unit cost and carton counts. Deciding late means requoting freight and sometimes redesigning the box.

  6. Ignoring compliance until customs

    If your product needs CPSIA testing, that is a two-week step. Discovering it at the port is not the moment.

  7. Not planning restock lead time

    Selling out is not a good outcome — it loses ranking. Reorder when you have roughly 10 weeks of stock left.

Choosing a Leather Product That Can Actually Win

We see the same product ideas repeatedly, and we can tell you which ones tend to go badly.

Categories that work

  • Cardholders and slim wallets. Low material cost, low FBA fulfilment fee (small and light), strong demand from a younger demographic, and genuine room to differentiate on leather grade and edge finish.
  • Belts. Our lowest minimum at {{pg:moq-from:Belt}} units, simple construction, and cut-to-size versions dramatically reduce SKU count — which matters enormously when you are managing FBA inventory across sizes.
  • Gift sets. A belt and wallet in matched leather commands a higher price than the parts separately and is difficult for a single-product competitor to answer.
  • Niche specifics. A wallet designed for a defined use case beats a generic wallet competing on price with a thousand identical listings.

Categories that tend to disappoint

  • Generic bifold wallets. The most saturated leather category on Amazon. Without a real differentiator you are competing purely on price and ad spend.
  • Large bags. High FBA fulfilment and storage fees, high return rates, expensive freight per unit, and higher MOQs. A poor first product almost universally.
  • Anything sized. Jackets mean a size curve, which means multiplying SKUs and inventory risk across sizes that sell at different rates.
  • Products relying on "genuine leather" as the selling point. It is a lower grade, buyers increasingly know it, and reviews punish it.
The FBA fee point most sourcing guides miss

Amazon's fulfilment fee is driven by size and weight tier. A cardholder and a large tote can have similar margins ex-works and completely different net margins after fulfilment and storage. Check the fee tier before you finalise dimensions — sometimes trimming a product by a few millimetres moves it into a cheaper tier, and that is a decision best made at tech pack stage rather than after tooling.

Restocking & Seasonal Planning

Going out of stock is more damaging than carrying inventory, because it costs ranking that then has to be rebought through advertising.

  1. Know your true replenishment lead time

    Repeat order production 20–30 days, sea freight 25–35 days, customs 3–7 days, FBA receiving 3–10 days. Call it 9–11 weeks door to available.

  2. Reorder at 10–12 weeks of remaining cover

    Not when you are running low. By then it is already too late.

  3. Plan Q4 in July

    Q4 inventory needs to be in FBA by early November. Working backwards, production has to start in August or September. Sellers who start in October pay for air freight or miss the quarter.

  4. Plan around factory closures

    Ramadan shortens working hours and Eid closes production for several days in Pakistan; Chinese New Year closes Chinese factories for two to four weeks. Ask your supplier for the calendar rather than discovering it.

  5. Split large restocks

    Two staggered shipments reduce storage fees and limit exposure if a batch has an issue.

Handling Quality Issues From Another Continent

Something will eventually go wrong. What matters is the process you agreed in advance.

  • Photograph everything on arrival, including cartons and labels, before units go into FBA.
  • Report immediately with evidence. A defect raised within days is a manufacturing conversation; one raised in month four is a negotiation.
  • Quantify the rate. "Some are bad" cannot be acted on. "11 of 200 have edge paint gaps, photos attached" can.
  • Know the remedy before you order. Ask any supplier what happens if a batch misses specification, and note whether the answer is specific. Ours is remake or credit against the archived approved sample.
  • Use pre-shipment inspection on early orders. SGS, Intertek or Bureau Veritas inspect before the goods leave — far cheaper than discovering a problem after freight and duty are spent.
  • Feed customer reviews back to the factory. Reviews are free QC data from real use. Most sellers never share them, and the factory then cannot fix the recurring issue.

How to Evaluate a Leather Manufacturer

  • Do they ask about your market and compliance needs unprompted? A supplier who never mentions CPSIA is not thinking about your risk.
  • Will they send physical swatches before you commit?
  • Can they explain the difference between full-grain and genuine leather clearly?
  • Do they say "CPSIA certified"? That is a red flag — it is batch testing, not a certificate.
  • Will they do an unscripted video call from the production floor?
  • Do they archive an approved reference sample for repeat-order consistency?
  • Are their payment terms sane — typically 30% deposit, 70% against copy bill of lading? Full 100% up front is a red flag.

Multi-Marketplace Considerations

If you sell in more than one Amazon marketplace, decide the split before production rather than after — labelling and compliance differ, and retrofitting either is expensive.

  • Separate FNSKU labels per marketplace. A unit labelled for amazon.com cannot simply be sent to amazon.co.uk. Tell us the split at production and we label each carton accordingly.
  • EU and UK require separate compliance. Post-Brexit, UK REACH and EU REACH are distinct regimes. Bilingual or multilingual labelling requirements also differ by country.
  • Country-of-origin marking — "Made in Pakistan" must appear as required by each destination, and the placement rules are not identical.
  • Split shipments at origin. Shipping one consolidated container and re-splitting it in-market usually costs more than shipping two correctly labelled consignments from Karachi.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum order for a first FBA product?

Ours start at {{pg:moq-lowest}} pieces, with wallets from {{pg:moq-from:Wallet}} and belts from {{pg:moq-from:Belt}}. Most Chinese factories start at 300–500 units per style, which is why low MOQ is the single most useful thing a first-time seller can find.

Can you ship directly to Amazon fulfilment centres?

Yes. We handle FNSKU labelling, polybagging with suffocation warnings, carton labelling and box content compliance in-factory. For a first order we usually recommend shipping to yourself or a prep centre first so you inspect before Amazon does.

Do I need CPSIA testing for leather goods?

It depends on the product and whether it is intended for children. Adult leather accessories often fall outside children's product rules, but the determination is yours to make with your compliance advisor. We produce to the relevant limits and arrange third-party batch testing through SGS or Intertek, issued in your name. Note that no factory can be "CPSIA certified" — it is per-batch testing.

How long from order to sellable inventory?

Roughly 10–14 weeks on a first order, including sampling, revision, production, freight, customs and FBA receiving. Repeat orders on an approved design run 6–8 weeks.

Should I ship by sea or air?

Sea for anything planned. Air only for emergency restocks where the cost of going out of stock exceeds the freight premium — which for a high-ranking listing it sometimes does.

Can you do custom packaging with my branding?

Yes — embossed logos, woven labels, care cards, kraft sleeves and rigid gift boxes, all built to satisfy FBA prep rules at the same time. Decide packaging before production, because it affects unit cost and carton counts.

What if the first production run has defects?

Raise it with photographs immediately. Every batch is QC-checked against the archived approved sample, and where a batch has genuinely missed specification we remake or credit. Ask any supplier this question before you order and note whether the answer is specific.
