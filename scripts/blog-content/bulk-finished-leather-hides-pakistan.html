Leather Types Available

Our hide catalogue runs to {{pg:total-hides}} live options. Rather than list every one, here is how the range breaks down and what each animal is actually good for.

Cowhide

The bulk of our stock, and the default for most applications. Cowhide gives you large, consistent panels — a full hide typically yields 45–55 square feet — with the fibre density to take heavy use. Thickness across our range runs from roughly 0.8mm for garment-weight material up to 4.5mm for belting and structural work.

Cowhide is what you want for bags, belts, footwear, upholstery and anything structural. It is also the most economical per square foot because supply is steady and hides are large.

Buffalo

Heavier and more pronounced in grain than cowhide, with a distinctive natural pebbling. Buffalo has excellent tear strength, which makes it the usual choice for work bags, heavy-duty straps and rugged outerwear. The trade-off is that the coarser grain is less suited to fine small leather goods where a smooth surface is the point.

Goat

Lightweight, unusually strong for its weight, and with a fine natural grain pattern. Goat is the standard choice for garment leather, linings, gloves and small leather goods where suppleness matters more than thickness. Hides are small — typically 6–9 square feet — so panel-heavy designs need more pieces and more careful nesting.

Sheep

The softest material we carry, used for garment leather and premium linings. Low tensile strength relative to cow or goat, so it is a surface and comfort material rather than a structural one.

Tanning Methods: Chrome, Vegetable & Crust

This is the decision that most often gets made badly, usually because a buyer compares two quotes on price without noticing they are for different materials.

Chrome-tanned

Tanned with chromium salts. Fast to produce — days rather than months — soft, flexible, water resistant, and available in any colour. Around 85% of global leather production is chrome-tanned, and it is the correct choice for garments, upholstery, most bags and footwear linings.

The compliance point that matters: chromium III is the tanning agent and is not restricted; chromium VI is a restricted substance under EU REACH and forms when chrome leather is badly processed or stored. Our chemistry is REACH-compliant and batch-tested for chromium VI. If a supplier cannot tell you the difference between chrome III and chrome VI, that tells you something.

Vegetable-tanned

Tanned with tannins from bark and plant matter, over weeks to months. Firmer, denser, and it tools, carves and burnishes. Vegetable-tanned leather develops the deep patina that heritage brands sell on. It is also more expensive, less colour-flexible, and less water resistant.

Use veg tan for belts, tooled goods, holsters, structural bag panels and anything where the leather should stiffen and darken with age. Do not use it for garments — it is too rigid.

Crust

Crust is tanned and dried but not yet finished — no final colour, no topcoat. Buyers take crust when they want to control the finishing themselves, or when they are shipping to a finisher closer to their market. It is cheaper per square foot but it is an intermediate product, not a ready-to-cut material.

Quoting like for like

When you compare two hide quotes, confirm all four variables before you compare price: animal, tanning method, thickness, and finish. A crust cowhide and a finished semi-aniline cowhide are both "cowhide" and the price gap between them is large and entirely legitimate.

Pricing Structure & Bulk Minimums

Hides are priced per square foot, and the rate depends on the four variables above plus grade. We quote against a specification rather than publishing a single rate, because a per-square-foot number without a spec attached is meaningless — and any supplier who gives you one without asking what you need is quoting a different material than the one you will receive.

What drives the rate

  • Grade. Hides are graded on the extent of natural markings — scars, insect bites, brand marks. Higher grades yield more usable panel area per hide, which is what you are actually buying.
  • Thickness and substance. Splitting to a tight tolerance costs more than supplying a natural substance range.
  • Finish complexity. Aniline and pull-up finishes require better raw material because there is no pigment layer to hide defects.
  • Colour. Standard colours run from stock. Custom colour matching requires a dye lot and carries a higher minimum.
  • Volume. Rates band down with quantity in the normal way.

Minimums

Minimum order quantities across the hide catalogue start low — individual lines are listed with their own minimums, and stock lines in standard colours can be supplied in small quantities for sampling and short runs. Custom colour matching carries a higher minimum because it requires a dedicated dye lot.

Shipping & Export Documentation

Leather is a straightforward export commodity, but it is not paperwork-free — hides are an animal product and customs authorities treat them accordingly.

What ships with every order

  • Commercial invoice and packing list with square footage per bundle.
  • Certificate of Origin — required for preferential duty treatment.
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill.
  • Phytosanitary / veterinary health certificate where the destination requires it for animal-origin goods.
  • Chemical compliance documentation — REACH declaration, and chromium VI test reports where requested.

Why Source Leather Hides from Pakistan

Three structural reasons, none of which is simply "it is cheap":

  1. Domestic raw material supply

    Pakistan has one of the largest livestock populations in the world, and a significant share of hides comes onto the market around Eid al-Adha. Tanneries buy raw material domestically rather than importing it, which removes a cost layer and an import lead time that competing origins carry.

  2. An established tanning cluster

    Kasur has been a tanning centre for generations, with the effluent treatment infrastructure and technical labour that implies. This is not a new industry chasing an export trend — the supply chain and the skills are already in place.

  3. EU GSP+ preferential access

    Pakistan holds GSP+ status with the European Union, giving qualifying leather goods reduced or zero duty on entry. For an EU buyer this changes landed cost in a way that a per-square-foot comparison alone will not show.

The honest counterpoint: shipping lead times from Karachi are longer than from East Asia to the US west coast, and Pakistan's tanning sector is more variable in quality than China's largest export tanneries. That variability is precisely why hide grading and batch documentation matter, and why we do both. We set this out in full in our Pakistan vs China sourcing comparison.

Understanding Hide Grades & Yield

Grade is the variable buyers understand least and pay for most. Hides are graded on the extent of natural markings — healed scars, insect bites, brand marks, growth lines. A higher grade means more usable panel area per hide.

You are buying yield, not appearance

A lower grade at a lower per-square-foot rate can cost more per finished piece, because you cut around more defects and throw away more material. The number that matters is cost per usable panel, not cost per square foot. Work out how many pieces you get from a hide at each grade before deciding the cheaper rate is cheaper.

Grade requirements also depend on finish. Aniline and pull-up finishes have no pigment layer to conceal markings, so they need better raw material. A pigmented or embossed finish tolerates a lower grade because the surface treatment covers minor defects — which is exactly why those finishes are more economical.

Nesting and cutting loss

Hides are irregular in shape. Even at high grade, cutting rectangular panels from a hide with a neck, belly and legs produces waste. Typical usable yield runs 65–80% depending on panel size and shape — large single panels waste more than small ones. Factor this into material planning: ordering exactly the square footage your panels total will leave you short.

Ordering for Consistency Across Repeat Batches

Leather is a natural material and dye lots vary. Managing that is a purchasing decision as much as a supplier one:

  • Order colour-critical quantities in a single dye lot. If you need exact match across a product range, buy the total requirement at once rather than splitting across repeat orders.
  • Keep a retained swatch. We hold one against your approved sample; hold your own too, so any dispute is settled against a physical reference rather than memory.
  • Specify a substance range, not a single figure. "1.2–1.4mm" is achievable and stable. "1.3mm exactly" is not how leather behaves, and insisting on it raises cost without improving anything.
  • Accept natural markings in writing. Agree upfront what level of natural marking is acceptable for your grade. This is the single most common source of disputes on hide orders, and it is entirely preventable.

Never buy bulk leather from a photograph. Screen colour is unreliable, and photographs tell you nothing about temper — how the leather breaks, drapes and folds — which is what determines whether it behaves in your production.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is leather priced — per hide or per square foot?

Per square foot. Hides vary in size, so a per-hide price would mean paying the same for a 40 sq ft hide as a 55 sq ft one. Your invoice states the total square footage supplied.

What is the difference between full-grain and top-grain?

Full-grain retains the intact outer grain surface with all its natural markings — the strongest and most durable form. Top-grain has had the surface lightly sanded to remove imperfections, giving a more uniform look at the cost of some strength. Our full-grain leather guide covers this in detail.

Can you match a specific colour?

Yes. Send a physical reference — a swatch, or the product you are matching to. Custom colour requires a dedicated dye lot and therefore a higher minimum than a stock colour.

Do you supply crust leather?

Yes, for buyers who finish in their own market. Note that crust is an intermediate product and requires finishing before it can be cut into goods.

How consistent is colour between batches?

Leather is a natural material and dye lots vary slightly. We grade and document per batch and hold a retained sample against your approved swatch. For colour-critical work, order the full quantity in one dye lot rather than splitting across repeat orders.

Is your leather REACH compliant?

Yes. Our chemistry complies with EU REACH restrictions including chromium VI, azo dyes and formaldehyde limits. Batch test reports are available on request and are issued in your name.
