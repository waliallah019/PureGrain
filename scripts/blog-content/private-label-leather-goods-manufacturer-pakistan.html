Our OEM Process: Tech Pack to Shipment

  1. Enquiry and specification

    Send a tech pack, a reference product, or photographs and a written description. We come back with feasibility, a leather recommendation, and an indicative price at your target quantity.

  2. Material selection

    We send physical swatches of the proposed leather. Colour and hand-feel cannot be judged on a screen, and approving material from photographs is the single most common cause of a rejected first shipment.

  3. Sampling

    We produce a physical pre-production sample. You approve it, or you mark it up and we revise. Nothing enters production until a sample is signed off in writing.

  4. Pre-production approval

    The approved sample becomes the reference standard. We photograph and archive it, and it is what the QC team measures the production run against.

  5. Production

    Cutting, skiving, stitching, edge finishing and assembly. In-line inspection at each stage rather than a single check at the end.

  6. Quality control

    Six-stage QC with in-house lab testing — thickness, tensile strength, colour fastness and chemical compliance. Details on our quality and testing page.

  7. Labelling and packaging

    Your brand labels, care cards, hangtags, polybags and cartons. For Amazon sellers this includes FNSKU labelling and carton-level shipment labels — see the FBA sourcing guide.

  8. Export and documentation

    Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, bill of lading, and any buyer-specific documents. We ship FOB Karachi as standard; CIF and DDP are available on request.

Certifications, Compliance & Quality Control

Compliance is where most sourcing conversations quietly fall apart, so here is our position stated plainly.

What we hold

  • ISO 9001 — quality management system, externally audited.
  • ISO 14001 — environmental management system.
  • LWG (Leather Working Group) — our tanning partners are LWG-certified, which is the audit standard most Western brands now require for leather sourcing.
  • REACH — our chemistry is compliant with EU restrictions on substances including chromium VI, azo dyes and formaldehyde.

What we support but do not "hold"

An honest distinction

CPSIA and California Proposition 65 are not certifications a factory can hold. They are US regulatory requirements met through product testing on a specific batch. Any supplier claiming to be "CPSIA certified" is either confused or misleading you. What we do is produce to the relevant limits and arrange third-party batch testing through SGS, Intertek or Bureau Veritas when your market requires it. That testing is quoted per batch and the report is issued in your name.

Six-stage quality control

Every batch passes through inspection at material intake, cutting, stitching, assembly, finishing and pre-shipment. Our in-house lab tests thickness consistency, tensile strength, colour fastness to rubbing and light, and chemical compliance. Hide grading is documented per batch, which is what lets us hold colour and temper consistent across a repeat order — the failure mode that ends most supplier relationships is not a bad first order, it is a second order that does not match the first.

Branding & Packaging Options

Private label means the product carries your identity, not ours. Nothing we ship under your brand carries any Pure Grain marking unless you ask for it.

On the product

  • Embossing and debossing — your logo pressed into the leather with a heated metal die. The die is a one-off tooling cost on the first order and is stored and reused free on every repeat order. Blind, foil and pigment fills available.
  • Woven and printed labels — stitched into interiors and linings. Low cost, high effect on perceived quality.
  • Custom metal hardware — branded buckles, zip pulls and engraved plates. Requires a mould or die on the first order and adds 15–25 days to that order only.
  • Edge paint in a brand colour — inexpensive, distinctive, and consistently underused.
  • Custom stitch colour — the cheapest brand signature available. Costs essentially nothing and is instantly recognisable.

Packaging

  • Polybag — protective minimum, with suffocation warnings where required.
  • Kraft sleeve or pouch — light, recyclable, good margin-to-impact ratio.
  • Rigid gift box — the strongest retail presentation and usually the largest single packaging cost.
  • Dust bags — cotton or non-woven, standard on bags and premium goods.
  • Hangtags, care cards and authenticity inserts — printed to your artwork.
  • Retail cartons with barcodes — including FNSKU and shipment labelling for Amazon sellers.
Decide packaging before production, not after

Packaging changes unit cost, carton dimensions and carton counts, which in turn changes freight cost. Deciding late means requoting freight and occasionally redesigning the box. It is the most common source of avoidable delay in a first order.

Repeat Orders & Consistency

First orders are relatively easy. The supplier relationships that fail, fail on the second or third order — and almost always for the same reason: the factory produced against a fresh reading of the tech pack rather than against the thing you actually approved.

  • The approved sample is archived as the physical reference standard and photographed in detail.
  • Hide grading is documented per batch, so leather selection for a repeat order targets the same grade and substance rather than whatever is convenient.
  • QC measures against the archived sample, not against the drawings.
  • Tooling is stored — embossing dies, cutting dies, hardware moulds and graded patterns are retained, so repeat orders carry no setup cost.
  • Colour-critical work should be ordered in one dye lot. Leather is natural and dye lots vary slightly; if exact match across a range matters, order the full quantity together rather than splitting across repeats.

Reorder planning

Repeat orders run 6–8 weeks for small leather goods and 8–10 weeks for bags and outerwear, plus freight. Work backwards from that: if you sell through in 12 weeks, place the reorder at around week 4. Going out of stock is more expensive than carrying inventory, particularly on marketplaces where availability drives ranking.

What We Do Not Do

Worth stating plainly, because a supplier who claims to do everything is telling you something about their reliability.

  • We do not produce bonded leather. It cracks and delaminates within a year and generates returns that outlast the saving.
  • We are not a single-site factory. We are a full-service OEM coordinating specialist units across Sialkot, Kasur and Karachi. That is why we can supply both hides and finished goods — and we would rather say so than show you one workshop and imply it makes everything.
  • We do not hold CPSIA or CE "certification". Neither is a factory certificate. We build to the relevant standards and coordinate third-party batch testing issued in your name.
  • We do not compete on synthetics. If your product is primarily PU or mixed synthetic materials at high volume, China's component ecosystem will serve you better. Our Pakistan vs China comparison sets out where each origin genuinely wins.
  • We do not quote without a specification. There is no price list for private label, because the price is a function of what you are making.
